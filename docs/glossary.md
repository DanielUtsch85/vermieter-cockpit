# Glossary

**Project:** Vermieter Cockpit DE *(working title)*  
**Version:** 0.2  
**Status:** Living Document

**MRE Integration Revision:** Aligned with `mre-integration.md`, `domain-model.md`, `data-model.md`, and `api-design.md`. Version 0.2 establishes the post-MRE-deep-dive canonical vocabulary.

---

# 1. Purpose

This glossary defines the canonical terminology used throughout Vermieter Cockpit DE.

Its purpose is to ensure that:

- product documentation uses consistent language
- architecture documents refer to the same concepts
- domain objects have stable meanings
- developers avoid ambiguous terminology
- AI coding agents interpret project terminology consistently

Where a term is defined here, this definition should be preferred over informal or colloquial usage elsewhere in the project.

---

# 2. Terminology Rules

## Canonical Names

Domain concepts should use the English canonical term defined in this document.

German legal or market-specific terms may be retained where translation would reduce precision.

Examples:

- `WEG`
- `Wirtschaftsplan`
- `Jahresabrechnung`
- `Sonderumlage`
- `AfA`

---

## Singular Concepts

Documentation should prefer singular entity names.

Preferred:

`Transaction`

Not:

`Transactions Object`

---

## Avoid Ambiguous Synonyms

Once a canonical term exists, competing synonyms should be avoided.

Example:

Use:

`Transaction Split`

Do not alternate between:

- Transaction Part
- Booking Split
- Payment Component
- Ledger Segment

unless they represent genuinely different concepts.

---

# 3. Actual

A confirmed historical fact representing something that has actually occurred.

Examples:

- a bank payment
- a confirmed rental contract / rental relationship
- an issued invoice
- a confirmed WEG Annual Statement

Actual data must remain distinct from Plan, Forecast and Scenario data.

---

# 4. Acquisition

A first-class Aggregate Root representing the economic acquisition of the real-world Property identified in DE through a `PropertyReference`.

It provides context for:

- purchase price
- acquisition-related costs
- financing
- ownership transition
- land/building allocation
- initial depreciation basis

An Acquisition is not the same as the Property itself.

---

# 5. Acquisition Cost

A cost economically associated with acquiring the real-world Property identified through a `PropertyReference`.

Examples may include:

- purchase price
- real estate transfer tax
- notary cost
- land registry cost
- broker fee

Where an Acquisition Cost originates from an actual cash payment, the underlying payment remains a canonical Transaction.

---

# 6. Actual Cashflow

A Cashflow derived from confirmed historical Transactions over a defined period.

It must not include hypothetical or projected movements.

---

# 7. AfA

**Absetzung für Abnutzung.**

The German tax concept of depreciation.

Within the platform, AfA is represented through the Depreciation domain.

AfA is a non-cash tax concept and is therefore not a Ledger Transaction.

---

# 8. Aggregate

A cluster of related domain objects with a defined consistency boundary.

An Aggregate is managed through an Aggregate Root.

Examples include:

- Acquisition Aggregate
- Loan Aggregate
- Transaction Aggregate
- WEG Aggregate

---

# 9. Aggregate Root

The primary Entity through which an Aggregate is accessed and modified.

Examples:

- Acquisition
- Loan
- Transaction
- WEG

---

# 10. AI Suggestion

A non-authoritative recommendation generated by an AI system.

Examples:

- proposed Financial Category
- proposed Tax Classification
- proposed document type
- proposed Transaction match

An AI Suggestion becomes canonical data only after an explicit domain action and required human review.

---

# 11. Allocation

The assignment of an amount, share or value to one or more domain objects.

Examples:

- Loan allocation between PropertyReferences
- acquisition cost allocation between land and building
- WEG cost allocation
- ownership allocation

The meaning of an Allocation must always be qualified by its domain.

---

# 12. Automation Rule

A deterministic rule that reduces repetitive work.

Examples:

- recognize recurring Hausgeld
- match known rental payments
- assign a known counterparty

Automation Rules must not bypass domain validation or invariants.

---

# 13. Bank Account

A `FinancialAccount` held at a banking institution.

Bank Accounts may be used as the source or destination of Transactions.

Where available, a FinancialAccount may contain an IBAN or external account identifier.

`Account` must not be used as shorthand for FinancialAccount because MRE `Account` means an authenticated MRE application user.

---

# 14. Canonical Data

Authoritative source data owned by exactly one domain.

Examples:

- Transaction
- Loan
- RentalContractReference
- Ownership
- Tax Classification

Canonical Data must not have competing independently editable copies.

---

# 15. Cashflow

Financial inflows and outflows over a defined period.

The term should be qualified where ambiguity exists.

Examples:

- Actual Cashflow
- Cashflow Before Financing
- Cashflow After Financing
- Cashflow Before Tax
- Cashflow After Tax

Cashflow is derived from underlying financial data.

---

# 16. Cash-on-Cash Return

An Investment Metric comparing relevant cash return to invested cash equity over a defined period.

Abbreviation:

`CoC` or `CCR`

The exact canonical formula must be versioned in the Investment specification.

---

# 17. Confirmed

A state indicating that information has been reviewed sufficiently to become authoritative within its domain.

Confirmed does not necessarily mean legally or professionally audited.

---

# 18. Correction

A traceable amendment to previously confirmed information.

Corrections are preferred over silent mutation where historical auditability matters.

---

# 19. Counterparty

The external Party associated with a financial Transaction.

Examples:

- tenant
- lender
- contractor
- WEG administrator
- insurer

---

# 20. Depreciation Asset

A tax-relevant asset or basis that is subject to depreciation.

Examples may include:

- depreciable building acquisition basis
- qualifying capitalized improvement

A Depreciation Asset is canonical input to a Depreciation Schedule.

---

# 21. Depreciation Schedule

A reproducible calculation of depreciation amounts over time.

It is derived from:

- Depreciation Asset
- applicable rule
- dates
- method
- ownership allocation

---

# 22. Derived Data

Information calculated from Canonical Data.

Examples:

- NOI
- Remaining Debt
- Equity
- IRR
- LTV

Derived Data may be cached or snapshotted, but must remain reproducible.

---

# 23. Document

A file with domain significance.

Examples:

- invoice
- rental contract / MRE rental document
- WEG Annual Statement
- loan agreement
- notarial purchase agreement
- bank statement

A Document may reference multiple domain Entities without being duplicated.

---

# 24. Domain

A clearly bounded area of business responsibility.

Current major Domains include:

- Property Management
- Financial Ledger
- Financing
- WEG
- Taxation
- Depreciation
- Investment
- Reporting
- Automation
- AI Assistance

Each mutable business concept has one authoritative Domain Owner.

---

# 25. Domain Owner

The Domain responsible for the authoritative lifecycle of a business concept.

Example:

`Financial Ledger` owns `Transaction`.

Other Domains may reference the Transaction but do not create competing versions.

---

# 26. DSCR

**Debt Service Coverage Ratio.**

An Investment Metric comparing relevant operating income to debt-service obligations.

The exact canonical formula is defined separately.

---

# 27. Economic Transfer Date

The date on which the economic benefits and burdens of a Property transfer between seller and buyer according to the transaction structure.

For German acquisitions, this may differ from:

- purchase contract date
- payment date
- land registry ownership date

The exact legal interpretation belongs to the applicable domain rules.

---

# 28. Entity

A domain object with persistent identity across time.

Examples:

- PropertyReference
- Party
- Loan
- Transaction

Two Entities may contain identical attributes while remaining distinct because they have different identities.

---

# 29. Equity

An Investment Metric representing the residual economic value after relevant debt is deducted from a selected Property value.

Conceptually:

`Equity = Property Value - Relevant Debt`

Because Property Value may be estimated, Equity may also contain estimation uncertainty.

---

# 30. External Reference

An identifier linking an internal Entity to another system or imported source.

Examples:

- MRE Realm ID + MRE Entity ID
- bank transaction ID
- tax software identifier

An External Reference must not normally replace the platform's internal identity.

---

# 31. Financial Category

The economic classification of a Transaction Split.

Examples:

- Rental Income
- Loan Interest
- Loan Principal
- Maintenance
- Administration
- Reserve Contribution

A Financial Category is not automatically a Tax Classification.

---

# 32. Financial Fact

An authoritative fact representing actual monetary activity.

In the current architecture, Financial Facts are represented by cash-based Transactions and Transaction Splits.

---

# 32A. FinancialAccount

A DE-owned Entity representing a source or destination of actual cash movements.

Examples:

- bank account
- Security Deposit account
- supported cash account

A FinancialAccount may carry external identifiers such as an IBAN.

A Loan is modeled separately in the Financing Domain and is not a FinancialAccount merely because a bank exposes a loan account number.

Do not confuse `FinancialAccount` with MRE `Account`, which represents an authenticated MRE user.

---

# 33. Financial Ledger

The Domain that owns canonical actual cash movements.

The Financial Ledger owns:

- Transactions
- Transaction Splits
- transaction provenance
- transaction classification state

The Ledger is not intended to become a general-purpose accounting system.

---

# 34. Financing

The Domain representing debt and financing contracts.

It owns concepts such as:

- Loan
- Loan Terms
- Loan Schedule
- refinancing

Actual payments remain owned by the Financial Ledger.

---

# 35. Forecast

A future estimate generated from current facts and assumptions.

Forecast differs from Plan because it may be calculated automatically rather than intentionally budgeted.

Forecast must remain separate from Actual.

---

# 36. Hausgeld

The recurring payment made by a condominium owner to the WEG.

Hausgeld may economically contain several components such as:

- recoverable operating costs
- non-recoverable costs
- administration
- reserve contribution

A Hausgeld bank payment may therefore require multiple Transaction Splits.

---

# 37. IBAN

**International Bank Account Number.**

Used as an external bank-account identifier where available.

IBAN is metadata, not internal Entity identity.

---

# 38. Investment Metric

A calculated measure used to evaluate PropertyReference or Portfolio performance.

Examples:

- NOI
- Cashflow
- IRR
- Cash-on-Cash Return
- LTV
- DSCR
- Equity

Investment Metrics are Derived Data.

---

# 39. Investment Metric Snapshot

A persisted, versioned record of a calculated Investment Metric at a specific point in time.

A Snapshot may preserve:

- metric version
- calculation date
- source period
- valuation reference
- scenario
- assumptions

Snapshots do not become independent financial truth.

---

# 40. Investment Scenario

A set of hypothetical assumptions used to evaluate possible future outcomes.

Examples:

- rent growth
- interest rates
- vacancy
- sale price
- property appreciation

Scenario data must never overwrite Actual data.

---

# 41. IRR

**Internal Rate of Return.**

An Investment Metric representing the discount rate at which the net present value of a defined series of investment cashflows equals zero.

The exact platform implementation and assumptions must be defined in the Investment specification.

---

# 42. Jahresabrechnung

The annual WEG statement allocating actual costs and financial activity for a completed financial period.

English project term:

`WEG Annual Statement`

Where legal or operational precision matters, `Jahresabrechnung` may be retained.

---

# 43. Jurisdiction

The legal or tax territory under which a Rule applies.

Initial primary Jurisdiction:

`DE`

Future country support must not require redesigning generic platform Domains.

---

# 44. Land and Building Allocation

The allocation of relevant Acquisition Cost between:

- land
- building

This allocation is particularly relevant for depreciation because land is generally not represented as a Depreciation Asset.

The allocation must preserve provenance.

---

# 45. Lease Definition

`Lease Definition` is the canonical project term for MRE `Lease`.

In the inspected MRE model, Lease is primarily a reusable definition of rental terms / contractual configuration.

It is **not** the complete concrete tenant contract.

The concrete operational rental relationship is centered on MRE Tenant together with its Property relationships, rent state and optional Lease Definition.

For DE-side references to that concrete relationship, use:

`RentalContractReference`

Do not use generic `Lease` as a synonym for the concrete rental relationship in new DE documentation or code.

---

# 46. Lender

A Party providing financing under a Loan.

Examples include:

- bank
- private lender
- financing institution

---

# 47. Loan

A financing contract representing borrowed capital and repayment obligations.

A Loan may be allocated to or secured by one or more real-world Properties referenced through PropertyReferences.

A Loan is not the same as the cash Transactions generated by its repayment.

---

# 48. Loan Allocation

The explicit attribution of a Loan or portions of a Loan to one or more PropertyReferences for financing and analytical purposes.

A shared Loan remains one canonical Loan.

Loan Allocation must not duplicate the Loan itself.

---

# 49. Loan Schedule

A representation of expected future financing payments.

A Loan Schedule may derive:

- expected payment
- expected interest
- expected principal
- expected Remaining Debt

A Loan Schedule represents expected behavior, not Actual payment history.

---

# 50. LTV

**Loan-to-Value.**

An Investment Metric comparing relevant debt with a selected Property or Portfolio valuation.

Because the denominator may be estimated, the metric should preserve its valuation reference.

---

# 51. Maintenance

Work performed to preserve, repair or restore a real-world Property or a component of it.

The precise tax treatment of Maintenance is not determined by the general term itself.

---

# 52. Maintenance Project

A Domain Entity grouping related expenditures and Documents belonging to one economic measure.

Examples:

- bathroom renovation
- heating repair
- flooring replacement

A Maintenance Project does not replace the underlying Transactions.

---

# 53. Maintenance Reserve

The reserve held by a WEG for future maintenance and repair of common property.

German term:

`Erhaltungsrücklage`

Reserve contribution and actual expenditure from the reserve are distinct concepts.

---

# 54. Market Valuation

A dated estimate of a Property's market value.

It should contain provenance such as:

- value
- date
- method
- source
- review state

A Market Valuation is an estimate, not a measured financial fact.

---

# 55. Miteigentumsanteil

The legal co-ownership share in the common property of a WEG.

This concept is distinct from the platform's Ownership Share in the investment itself.

It belongs to the WEG domain.

---

# 56. MRE

**MicroRealEstate.**

The upstream open-source rental-management application used as the operational core of Vermieter Cockpit DE.

MRE remains authoritative for operational rental-management concepts including:

- `Property` — the rentable operational object
- `Tenant` — the central record for the concrete rental relationship
- `Lease` / Lease Definition — reusable rental-term configuration
- rent obligations and operational payment state
- rental-management Documents

The inspected MRE core does **not** expose a separate canonical `Unit` entity.

The project should extend MRE rather than modify it wherever reasonably possible.

MRE-specific semantics are translated through the DE integration boundary instead of being duplicated or reinterpreted inside DE domains.

---

# 57. NOI

**Net Operating Income.**

An Investment Metric representing income generated by property operations before financing effects.

The exact canonical formula must be defined in the Investment specification.

NOI is Derived Data.

---

# 58. Non-Cash Event

An economically relevant event that does not represent an actual cash movement.

Examples:

- AfA
- unrealized property appreciation
- projected interest

Non-Cash Events are not stored as Ledger Transactions under the current architecture.

---

# 59. Operating Cost Allocation

The allocation of operating expenses to the Tenant context represented through `RentalContractReference` for rental-cost settlement purposes.

This is distinct from:

- Financial Category
- Tax Classification
- WEG Cost Line

---

# 60. Ownership

The temporal relationship between a Party and the real-world Property identified through `PropertyReference`, representing legal ownership.

Ownership contains the canonical Ownership Share.

---

# 61. Ownership Share

The legal ownership percentage assigned to an Owner for a defined validity period.

Examples:

- 100%
- 50%
- 20%

All default owner-level allocation within the MVP derives from Ownership Share.

Separate economic or tax shares are not modeled by default.

---

# 62. Owner

A Party holding an active Ownership relationship with a PropertyReference.

Owner is a role of Party, not a separate identity model.

---

# 63. Party

The canonical representation of a Person or Organization interacting with the platform.

A Party may hold multiple roles.

Examples:

```text id="nps7eu"
Party
├── Owner
├── Borrower
└── Landlord
```

Party identity should not be duplicated independently across Domains.

---

# 64. Person

A Party representing a natural person.

---

# 65. Organization

A Party representing a legal entity, association, bank, company or other organization.

---

# 66. Plan

An intentionally entered expectation for a future period.

Examples:

- planned rent
- maintenance budget
- planned operating costs

Plan must remain separate from Actual.

---

# 67. Portfolio

A logical collection of PropertyReferences analyzed together.

A PropertyReference may exist without a Portfolio.

Portfolio membership does not change underlying financial truth.

---

# 68. Property

`Property` means the MRE-owned rentable operational object.

For the initial condominium use case, an MRE Property represents the apartment that is rented and operationally managed.

MRE Property is the operational rental-management concept. DE-specific acquisition, ownership, financing, tax and investment facts are not added directly to it.

Important:

`MRE Property.price` is rental pricing metadata. It is not acquisition price and not Market Valuation.

DE domains refer to MRE Property through `PropertyReference`.

---

# 68A. PropertyReference

The canonical DE-side reference to an MRE-owned Property.

A PropertyReference preserves:

- internal DE reference identity
- MRE Realm identity
- MRE Property identity
- optional non-authoritative cached display metadata
- upstream reference state

DE-owned entities use `propertyReferenceId`, not a generic `propertyId`.

A PropertyReference does not duplicate the full MRE Property.

---

# 69. Property Management

The Domain concerned with rental operations.

Primary owner:

MicroRealEstate.

Responsibilities include:

- Property
- Tenant rental relationship
- Lease Definitions
- rent obligations / operational settlement state
- rental operations
- communication

---

# 70. Property Value

A valuation of the real-world Property, identified through PropertyReference, used for Investment calculations.

Property Value should always identify its source.

Possible sources include:

- acquisition price
- Market Valuation
- Scenario valuation

The phrase should not imply that one universally correct value exists.

---

# 71. Provenance

Information describing where a fact, Document, classification or calculation originated.

Examples:

- manual entry
- bank CSV
- API import
- MRE
- AI Suggestion followed by user confirmation
- tax Rule
- advisor confirmation

Provenance is a key requirement for auditability.

---

# 72. Recoverable Cost

A cost classified as eligible for allocation to a Tenant under the applicable rental-cost framework.

German colloquial concept:

`umlagefähige Kosten`

Recoverability and tax deductibility are independent classifications.

---

# 73. Remaining Debt

A derived Financing value representing outstanding Loan principal at a defined date.

Remaining Debt is not an independently editable financial source value.

---

# 74. Rent

A payment obligation arising from the concrete MRE rental relationship.

Where precision is required, distinguish:

- Base Rent
- Operating-Cost Advance
- Total Rent Obligation
- Actual Rent Payment

---

# 75. Rent Obligation

An expected contractual amount owed under the concrete MRE rental relationship.

Rent Obligation is not a Transaction until actual money moves.

---

# 75A. RentalContractReference

The canonical DE integration reference to the concrete rental relationship owned by MRE.

It primarily references:

- MRE Realm
- MRE Tenant
- optional MRE Lease Definition
- PropertyReference(s)
- relevant Party mapping(s)

`RentalContractReference` contains references, not duplicated rental truth.

Use `rentalContractReferenceId` in DE-owned entities that need to refer to the concrete rental relationship.

---

# 75B. RentPaymentReconciliation

An explicit DE integration Entity linking MRE operational rent-payment state with an actual DE Ledger Transaction or Transaction Split.

It connects two distinct sources of truth:

```text
MRE operational rent state
          │
          ▼
RentPaymentReconciliation
          │
          ▼
DE Ledger actual cash
```

A reconciliation does not create a Transaction and does not turn an MRE payment record into canonical cash truth.

Matching may be suggested automatically, but confirmation and MRE write-back remain explicit and auditable.

---

# 76. Rental Income

A Financial Category representing cash received as income from rental activity.

Security Deposits must not be classified as Rental Income.

---

# 77. Reporting

The Domain responsible for presenting information through:

- dashboards
- exports
- PDF reports
- annual summaries

Reporting does not own authoritative business calculations.

---

# 78. Reserve Contribution

A Transaction Split or financial classification representing cash contributed to a WEG Maintenance Reserve.

A Reserve Contribution must not automatically be interpreted as an immediate tax expense.

---

# 79. Review State

The explicit certainty or approval status of interpretive data.

Possible conceptual states include:

- Unreviewed
- Suggested
- Confirmed
- Needs Review
- Advisor Confirmed

Exact workflows may differ by Domain.

---

# 80. Rule

A versioned business, legal, tax or calculation instruction used to interpret data.

Important Rule attributes may include:

- Jurisdiction
- version
- valid-from
- valid-until
- source

Rules must remain testable and traceable.

---

# 81. Scenario

See `Investment Scenario`.

Scenario is hypothetical data and must not be confused with Forecast or Actual.

---

# 82. Security Deposit

A dedicated Domain Entity representing a rental deposit obligation and its financial lifecycle.

German term:

`Kaution`

It may contain or reference:

- RentalContractReference
- expected amount
- payer Party
- holding FinancialAccount
- IBAN / external account identifier
- actual receipt Transaction(s)
- actual repayment Transaction(s)
- relevant dates
- reconciliation state

MRE `Tenant.guaranty` and `guarantyPayback` are operational rental-management values. They may seed or reconcile expected deposit state but are not canonical evidence of actual cash receipt or repayment.

Actual movement of deposit funds is represented through Ledger Transactions.

Security Deposit is not Rental Income.

---

# 83. Self-Hosting

Deployment of the complete platform under the user's operational control.

Self-hosting is a first-class architectural requirement.

---

# 84. Snapshot

A persisted representation of Derived Data at a defined point in time.

Snapshots must preserve sufficient context to remain interpretable and reproducible.

See:

`Investment Metric Snapshot`

---

# 85. Sonderumlage

An extraordinary WEG contribution outside normal recurring Hausgeld.

English project term:

`Special Assessment`

A Special Assessment may relate to a specific project or funding need.

Actual payment remains a Ledger Transaction.

---

# 86. Source Reference

A link from a domain concept to evidence or an external source supporting it.

Examples:

- Document
- bank import identifier
- applicable Rule
- advisor decision

---

# 87. Tax Classification

The tax interpretation assigned to an existing economic item.

Possible source items include:

- Transaction Split
- Acquisition Cost
- Maintenance Project
- WEG Cost Line

Tax Classification does not create financial cash movements.

---

# 88. Tax Rule

A versioned Rule governing tax interpretation for a defined Jurisdiction and validity period.

---

# 89. Taxation

The Domain responsible for tax interpretation.

Taxation interprets Canonical Data.

It does not maintain a competing financial ledger.

---

# 90. Tenant

In MRE, `Tenant` is the central operational record for the concrete rental relationship.

It may contain or reference:

- occupants
- rented Properties
- Lease Definition
- rent state
- operational payment information

A Tenant is therefore **not** modeled in DE simply as a Party holding a tenant role.

Where DE domains need canonical person or organization identity, they use `Party` and external mappings.

Where DE domains need to reference the concrete MRE rental relationship, they use `RentalContractReference`.

Operational Tenant ownership remains with MRE.

---

# 91. Transaction

The canonical representation of an actual cash movement.

Domain Owner:

Financial Ledger.

A Transaction may contain one or more Transaction Splits.

---

# 92. Transaction Split

An economically distinct component of a Transaction.

Example:

```text id="s04o6m"
Loan Payment
├── Interest
└── Principal
```

For a confirmed Transaction:

`Sum(Transaction Splits) = Transaction Amount`

---

# 93. Unit — Deferred

A separate canonical `Unit` entity is not part of the MVP model.

The inspected MRE core does not expose a separate Unit entity; MRE Property itself is the rentable operational object.

Terms such as apartment, parking space, garage or storage space may describe real-world components, but they must not cause `Unit`, `UnitReference`, `unitId` or `unitIds` to be introduced into MVP domain logic.

If a future multi-asset hierarchy is required, it should be designed explicitly through a future `Asset` / `Building` model and ADR.

---

# 94. Utility Settlement

A periodic operating-cost settlement between landlord and Tenant.

German context:

`Nebenkostenabrechnung` or `Betriebskostenabrechnung`, depending on intended scope.

It may use:

- actual allocated costs
- advance payments
- RentalContractReference / MRE rental information
- occupancy period

Underlying Transactions remain unchanged.

---

# 95. Value Object

An immutable domain concept identified by its value rather than persistent identity.

Examples:

- Money
- Percentage
- Date Range

---

# 96. WEG

**Wohnungseigentümergemeinschaft.**

The German condominium owners' association connected to a Property.

WEG is a dedicated German Domain concept and must remain outside generic MRE Property logic.

---

# 97. WEG Annual Statement

See `Jahresabrechnung`.

A record of actual WEG cost allocation for a completed financial period.

---

# 98. WEG Cost Line

An economically distinct line item within a WEG Economic Plan or Annual Statement.

A WEG Cost Line may later receive separate:

- financial classification
- tenant-recoverability classification
- Tax Classification

---

# 99. WEG Economic Plan

The WEG `Wirtschaftsplan`.

A planning document describing expected WEG income, costs and contributions for a future or current financial period.

A WEG Economic Plan represents Plan data, not Actual financial truth.

---

# 100. WEG Membership

The relationship between a PropertyReference and a WEG.

It may contain:

- Miteigentumsanteil
- validity period
- association reference
- relevant allocation information

---

# 101. Wirtschaftsplan

See `WEG Economic Plan`.

---

# 102. Working Title

`Vermieter Cockpit DE` is the current project name.

It is explicitly a working title and may be replaced without changing product identity or architectural meaning.

# 104. Preferred Vocabulary

Use:

`Financial Ledger`

instead of:

`Bookkeeping`

when referring to the project's canonical cash-movement domain.

Use:

`Transaction`

instead of:

`Booking`

unless discussing an external accounting system.

Use:

`Property`

only for the MRE-owned rentable operational object.

Use:

`PropertyReference`

for the canonical DE-side reference to that MRE Property.

Do not introduce:

- `Unit`
- `UnitReference`
- `unitId`
- `unitIds`

into MVP domain logic.

Use:

`Lease Definition`

when referring to MRE `Lease`.

Use:

`RentalContractReference`

when DE code or documentation refers to the concrete MRE rental relationship.

Use:

`FinancialAccount`

for bank/cash-account semantics.

Do not shorten FinancialAccount to `Account`, because MRE `Account` means an authenticated MRE user.

Use:

`Party`

for shared Person/Organization identity.

Use:

`Ownership Share`

for legal investment ownership.

Use:

`Miteigentumsanteil`

only for the WEG-specific common-property share.

Use:

`Actual`

for confirmed historical facts.

Use:

`Scenario`

only for deliberately hypothetical investment assumptions.

The following terminology is normative across product documentation, architecture, APIs, data models and implementation:

| Concept | Canonical Term |
|---|---|
| MRE rentable object | `Property` |
| DE reference to MRE rentable object | `PropertyReference` |
| Separate rentable sub-unit | Deferred; no MVP `Unit` |
| MRE reusable rental-term model | `Lease Definition` |
| DE reference to concrete MRE rental relationship | `RentalContractReference` |
| DE bank/cash account | `FinancialAccount` |
| Authenticated MRE user | `MRE Account` |
| MRE rent-state ↔ DE cash matching | `RentPaymentReconciliation` |

The superseded generic model:

`Property → Unit → Lease → Tenant`

must not be reintroduced into MVP documentation or code.

# 105. Terms to Avoid Without Qualification

The following terms are too ambiguous to use without additional context:

## Return

Specify:

- Cash-on-Cash Return
- IRR
- rental yield
- equity return

---

## Cashflow

Specify the relevant scope where necessary.

---

## Cost

Specify whether it refers to:

- cash outflow
- operating expense
- Acquisition Cost
- tax-deductible expense
- WEG Cost
- planned cost

---

## Value

Specify the valuation source.

---

## Owner Share

Prefer the canonical term:

`Ownership Share`

---

## Reserve

Prefer:

`Maintenance Reserve`

when referring to the WEG Erhaltungsrücklage.

---

## Account

Do not use unqualified `Account`.

Specify:

- `FinancialAccount` for DE bank/cash accounts
- `MRE Account` for authenticated MRE users

---

## Lease

Do not use unqualified `Lease` for the concrete rental relationship.

Specify:

- `Lease Definition` for MRE Lease
- `RentalContractReference` for the DE-side reference to the concrete MRE rental relationship

---

## Property ID

DE-owned entities should use:

`propertyReferenceId`

Raw MRE identity should use:

`mrePropertyId`

Do not use generic `propertyId` in new DE-owned entities.

# 106. Final Terminology Principle

Terms in this project should describe **what a concept means**, not merely how it is stored or displayed.

Where ambiguity exists, choose terminology that preserves the distinction between:

- reality
- financial representation
- tax interpretation
- investment analysis

The vocabulary of the system is part of its architecture.
